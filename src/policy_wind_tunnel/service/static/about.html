How it works

How it works, and what to trust

One contract per rule powers every screen. This page says what that means, what has been checked against official pages, and where the limits are.

The idea

A rule written in words becomes an exact contract

A scheme's published rule is turned into a short list of exact conditions. Each condition keeps the sentence it came from. The same contract then does three jobs.

  1. Answers citizens
    It decides a person's case and shows the sentence that applied. A solver finds the smallest change that would alter the answer.
  2. Simulates a change
    Officials re-decide thousands of synthetic applicants under a new rule and see who moves.
  3. Tests the software
    For every condition it builds two nearly identical people on either side of the line and checks the software agrees.
A person approves any unclear wording before it counts as a bug. A language model may draft a contract, but it never decides an answer: plain code evaluates the contract.

Limits

Read these before relying on anything

  • Not an official decision. Results are indicative. The issuing office decides.
  • Bundled people are fictional. The demo cards are invented. When Gemini web search is connected, the “My area” tab can look up real representatives. Those results are not reviewed by a person: each fact links to the page it came from, and anything without a matching source is left out.
  • No ratings. Live lookup shows offices held, committees, bills and terms. It never shows assets, criminal cases, contact details or any opinion, and it refuses questions that ask it to judge a person.
  • The population is synthetic. What-if percentages show how the mechanism works. They are not forecasts, and no behaviour is predicted.
  • Two simulation modes, different claims. The exact what-if re-decides every synthetic applicant under the changed rule — every number is traceable. The exploratory impact model uses PolicySim’s causal engine (MIT declared, vendored with attribution) to propagate rule changes through a causal graph whose edge weights come from our synthetic population. The exploratory numbers are model assumptions, not predictions of real-world effect. They are labelled “Exploratory only” throughout.
  • The benchmark is self-made. The planted bugs, the hand-written suite and the generator were written by the same team, so the numbers show recall on known kinds of bug only.
  • Some content is partial. Several countries have few verified entries. The citizen pages say which parts are unverified.
  • Nothing you enter is stored. Answers are evaluated on request and not saved.

Built with

Google Cloud and open tools

A Python and FastAPI service, designed to run on Google Cloud Run and not deployed yet. A Z3 solver does the boundary tests and gap checks, Gemini drafts contracts and runs the live area lookup, and PolicySim’s causal engine powers the exploratory impact model.
